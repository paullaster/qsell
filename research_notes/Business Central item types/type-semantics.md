# Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central item-type semantics

## Operational meaning, master data, and transaction behavior

### Takeaway
The Item Card **Type** is the operational distinction: Inventory tracks physical stock and its value; Non-Inventory represents a physical good deliberately excluded from inventory/availability and inventory-cost calculations; Service represents labor/time or other services. Item master cards, templates, and much of the commercial-document interface are shared, but inventory-ledger-dependent functionality is not equivalent across types.

### Cited Findings
- Business Central describes items broadly as goods or services traded by a business, maintained on an **Item Card**. The Type field governs how much the item is managed in inventory: Inventory is a physical unit managed/tracked in inventory; Non-Inventory is a physical unit not managed/tracked in inventory; Service is a labor-time unit typically used to register sales or purchases of services. [Create item cards for goods or services](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-register-new-items) (Microsoft Learn `ms.date` 2026-04-07; current rolling documentation, retrieved 2026-11-01).
- Standard item master entry is through **Items** list and **Item Card**; templates can prepopulate settings. The documented card setup includes General, Replenishment, Planning, Warehouse, Item Tracking, costing, dimensions, units of measure, variants, vendor and pricing information. [Create item cards for goods or services](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-register-new-items) (2026-04-07).
- The Replenishment FastTab can identify Purchase Order, Assembly Order, or Production Order supply. Planning and warehouse fields supplement this setup; warehouse class and put-away template are examples of warehouse data. The article explains these as item setup fields but does not claim all are relevant/enabled for all three Type values. [Create item cards for goods or services](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-register-new-items) (2026-04-07).
- Non-Inventory is a regular item-card type, not the same thing as a **catalog item**. Catalog items are not managed in the item list until sale, and are converted into a regular item when selected on a sales order, quote, or blanket order; they cannot be selected directly on a sales invoice. Non-Inventory remains a normal item, excluded from availability/costing calculations. [Create and manage catalog items](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-work-nonstock-items) (2024-11-21; updated 2025-08-21).
- The physical-products inventory flow explicitly uses Inventory-type item cards. Purchases and sales update inventory quantities; transfers update quantity across locations. Inventory transactions create item value entries, and inventory costs are posted to related G/L accounts; cost adjustment applies inbound purchase costs to outbound sales transactions. [Managing inventory](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-manage-inventory) (2026-04-07).
- Item costing methods determine valuation and the cost of goods sold; Business Central describes five methods (FIFO, LIFO, Specific, Average, Standard) in linked design documentation and says the item’s Costing Method is set on its card. [Create item cards for goods or services](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-register-new-items) (2026-04-07); [Managing inventory costs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/finance-manage-inventory-costs) (2026-07-21).
- Item tracking codes, assigned on the **Item Card** Item Tracking FastTab, define serial/lot/package tracking policies; they support item traceability through inbound/outbound documents and warehouse movements. Reservations are described in terms of inventory or inbound item quantities for sales, purchase, service, assembly, transfer, and production orders; specific reservations can bind a demand to serial/lot numbers. These are inventory-linked mechanisms, not proof that Non-Inventory or Service items have reservable stock. [Set up item tracking with serial, lot, and package numbers](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-setup-item-tracking) (2025-07-14); [How to reserve items](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-to-reserve-items) (2025-06-10).
- Warehouse handling is a separate configuration/flow from the item type alone. For the basic **Inventory Pick** method, picking can post shipment and warehouse entries; documentation explicitly says preview posting does not show entries for non-inventory source-document lines, giving G/L Account lines and Service items as examples. Service orders are outbound warehouse sources but do not support the basic order-by-order Inventory Pick complexity. [How to pick items with inventory picks](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/warehouse-how-to-pick-items-with-inventory-picks) (2026-09-04).
- Assembly is for sellable items with an assembly BOM and requires output and component consumption postings; assembly integrates with sales, planning, reservations, and warehousing. Assembly orders link component consumption and item output, and assemble-to-order can connect supply directly to sales. A type comparison table declaring that Non-Inventory/Service can never be assembly components or that assembly is exclusive to Inventory was not located in the reviewed sources. [Assembly Management](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/assembly-assemble-items) (2025-06-10); [Create item cards for goods or services](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-register-new-items) (2026-04-07).

### Inferences
- Operationally choose Inventory when accurate on-hand quantity, availability, replenishment planning, item tracking, warehouse execution, and inventory valuation/COGS are required. Choose Non-Inventory when a physical good is commercially represented as an item but inventory quantity/availability/costing must be bypassed. Choose Service for service/labor quantities. This selection guidance synthesizes Microsoft's stated definitions; local tax/posting setup still controls G/L accounts.
- Service appearing on a sales or purchase transaction does not imply stock movement. The warehouse-pick documentation specifically excludes Service lines from inventory pick posting preview, consistent with its labor/service semantics.
- Costing, warehouse and tracking facilities should be explained as Inventory-related capabilities rather than assumed as identical merely because all three types use Item Cards or appear as document line items.

### Gaps
- The direct **About Item Types** page linked by Microsoft's current item-card and catalog-item articles (`inventory-about-item-types`) was not retrievable in this research session (404). Thus detailed, explicit per-field/type matrices could not be verified from that primary overview.
- Microsoft Learn pages consulted generally do not document every validation rule for whether each type can be replenished, included in BOMs, reserved, tracked, or posted on every sales/purchase document. Avoid treating absence of a prohibition in task documentation as proof of support.
- No explicit universal G/L account mapping by item type was found. Posting groups and company setup determine accounts; the sources establish inventory cost posting, but not that Service/Non-Inventory always post to a particular expense or revenue account.

## Sales, purchase, posting, and document/page behavior

### Takeaway
All three types are represented through item master data and commercial document lines, but an Item line’s shared surface should not be mistaken for shared ledger effects. Inventory posting changes stock and value; Non-Inventory is explicitly excluded from availability and costing calculations; Service is used for service/labor transactions and does not behave as stock in warehouse picking.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft identifies **Item Card**, **Items**, and item templates as the standard item master setup pages; the item card covers goods and services and exposes Type selection. [Create item cards for goods or services](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-register-new-items) (2026-04-07).
- The catalog-item workflow demonstrates commercial line selection explicitly: on a sales order/quote/blanket order, choose line Type **Item**, leave No. blank, then use **Select Catalog Items**; conversion creates and selects a normal item. Catalog items cannot be selected from the **Sales Invoice** page. This flow concerns catalog items, not Non-Inventory type. [Create and manage catalog items](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-work-nonstock-items) (2024-11-21).
- Sales and purchase documents are listed as inventory-affecting flows for inventory items; transfers and journals also change inventory, with inventory transactions creating value entries and related G/L cost entries. [Managing inventory](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-manage-inventory) (2026-04-07).
- Cost posting can be configured with **Automatic Cost Posting** and **Expected Cost Posting to G/L** on **Inventory Setup**; inventory periods, item costing methods, cost adjustment and G/L reconciliation are related controls. [Managing inventory costs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/finance-manage-inventory-costs) (2026-07-21).
- Standard selling/assembly-related document and page names in the documentation include **Sales Quote**, **Sales Order**, **Blanket Sales Order**, **Sales Invoice**, **Assembly Order**, and **Inventory Pick**. The assembly-to-order flow can create a linked assembly order from sales demand and post assembly output, component consumption, and shipment together. [Create and manage catalog items](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-work-nonstock-items) (2024-11-21); [Assembly Management](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/assembly-assemble-items) (2025-06-10); [How to pick items with inventory picks](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/warehouse-how-to-pick-items-with-inventory-picks) (2026-09-04).
- The **Inventory Pick** document supports sales orders, purchase return orders and outbound transfer orders in basic warehouse configuration; posting posts source-document shipment and, for bin locations, warehouse entries. Service orders are outbound sources but not supported at this basic order-by-order complexity. Service item lines are cited as examples of non-inventory lines not represented in inventory-pick posting preview. [How to pick items with inventory picks](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/warehouse-how-to-pick-items-with-inventory-picks) (2026-09-04).
- Standard reservation pages include **Reservation**, **Reservation Entries**, and **Reservation Worksheet**. The cited procedure reserves item quantities for sales and similar purchase, service, transfer, and assembly orders; specific serial/lot reservation depends on item tracking. [How to reserve items](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-to-reserve-items) (2025-06-10).

### Inferences
- For each type, distinguish the document line’s commercial presence (quantity, description, price, vendor/customer) from downstream inventory/value ledger effects. Type selection and posting groups/setup jointly determine those effects.
- Service orders and Service item lines are related but distinct concepts: service order warehouse limitations in the cited page do not establish that every Service-type item uses the full Service Management workflow.
- A Non-Inventory item can be used as a regular item reference on a trade line, but the supported transaction-specific behaviors should be confirmed against the active BC version and setup, rather than inferred from catalog item or inventory-item instructions.

### Gaps
- The reviewed documentation does not provide a complete matrix of accepted item types for sales quote/order/invoice/return, purchase quote/order/invoice/return, service documents, journals, and each posting action.
- Exact G/L entries for Service and Non-Inventory lines depend on general/product posting groups and local setup; no definitive mapping by type alone was documented in the retrieved articles.
- No versioned release number (for example, Business Central 2026 release wave 2) was stated on these rolling Learn pages. They are current Learn content as retrieved 2026-11-01; use the article update dates above, and validate behavior against the tenant’s application version/localization.

## Assembly, inventory controls, and version/source applicability

### Takeaway
Assembly BOMs and assembly orders are inventory-oriented mechanisms: they create output and consume components, can link to sales demand, and integrate with planning/reservations/warehouse processes. Microsoft’s current item type description is clear at the semantic level, but some type-specific edge validations require checking in the target tenant because the accessible official material did not expose a full item-type compatibility matrix.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft describes an assembly item as a sellable item containing an assembly BOM. Assembly orders post both output and consumption, and integrate with sales, planning, reservations, and warehousing. [Assembly Management](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/assembly-assemble-items) (2025-06-10).
- The **Work with Bills of Material** path is linked from item management as the way to structure parent items and components for assembly and production BOMs. The Item Card article describes BOM hierarchies and identifies assembly and production as distinct replenishment systems. [Managing inventory](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-manage-inventory) (2026-04-07); [Create item cards for goods or services](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-register-new-items) (2026-04-07).
- For assemble-to-order sales, linked assembly supply can be created from sales order line demand; posting an inventory pick can create component movement and post assembly output plus sales shipment. [Assembly Management](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/assembly-assemble-items) (2025-06-10); [How to pick items with inventory picks](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/warehouse-how-to-pick-items-with-inventory-picks) (2026-09-04).
- The item-card article states that an item card with posted transactions cannot be deleted because ledger entries may be needed for inventory valuation/audit. [Create item cards for goods or services](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-register-new-items) (2026-04-07).
- Microsoft Learn article metadata indicates a rolling documentation state, not a fixed major-version moniker: the sources are identified as Dynamics 365 Business Central, and dates range from 2024-11-21 through 2026-09-04 (or later updated timestamps). The warehouse page was published 2026-09-04, so applicability reflects Learn content current at retrieval date 2026-11-01, not an assertion about an unreleased build or a particular on-premises cumulative update. [Inventory item setup](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-register-new-items); [Inventory pick](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/warehouse-how-to-pick-items-with-inventory-picks); [Assembly Management](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/assembly-assemble-items).

### Inferences
- The safest design rule is to treat Type as a contract about lifecycle and ledgers, not merely a label: whether the business needs stock quantity, stock valuation/COGS, item tracking/reservations, or warehouse work should drive the type, with applicable replenishment and posting setup layered on top.
- Assembly use should generally be modeled with Inventory-type parent/components when physical stock/output/consumption and valuation matter. The sources establish assembly mechanics but do not, in the accessible text, categorically rule out every other type as a component; confirm the actual allowed BOM line validation in the target build.

### Gaps
- Directly linked official reference [About Item Types](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-about-item-types) was unavailable (404), and this is the likely source for explicit differences across Inventory, Non-Inventory, and Service. No claim here substitutes for its absent text.
- The exact behavior of Non-Inventory in replenishment planning, reservations, item tracking, BOM validation, warehouse documents, item charges, and posting was not fully stated in the accessible official articles. Further tenant/version-specific verification is warranted for these edge cases.
- No dated release-specific Microsoft version selector/moniker was exposed by fetched article metadata. Therefore this note identifies the exact article dates and retrieval date, but does not assign a precise SaaS release or on-premises build number.
