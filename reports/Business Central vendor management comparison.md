# Strengthen qsell’s Vendor Lifecycle Controls

qsell already implements a broad vendor lifecycle—from staff and portal onboarding through qualification, RFQs, purchasing, supplier invoices, payables, payments, and controlled bank changes—but it is not a like-for-like replacement for the full Business Central procure-to-pay and finance platform. Business Central’s strongest comparative advantages are mature purchasing/payables accounting, configurable posting/tax/currency defaults, payment execution and reconciliation, and established reporting; qsell differentiates through supplier self-service, invitation/team access, sealed sensitive data, due diligence, collaborative RFQs, and locally relevant Kenya/ETIMS workflows. The reported inability to update a vendor profile is not established by repository evidence: the portal has profile save actions, but role and lifecycle gates intentionally limit which sections are editable and when. The invitation email concern has a plausible code-level explanation—the email adapter defaults to console unless SMTP is explicitly selected—but deployment settings and actual delivery have not been inspected. Since invitation-led onboarding is the desired policy, the principal policy change is to disable public vendor self-registration while preserving staff-created vendors and invitation acceptance. The implementation priorities are to verify the actual user journey and mail deployment first, then make invitation, profile-state, control, and documentation gaps explicit and testable.

## Business Central 26–29 emphasizes controlled payables, not a supplier portal

Business Central represents suppliers as vendor cards created by staff, from templates, or from contact records. The card supplies defaults to purchase documents and payments, while bank accounts and remit-to/order addresses are related records. Posting groups and setup tables map payables and purchase/tax combinations to ledger accounts; currency, terms, and payment method refer to centrally configured setup. Purchase orders support partial receipt and invoicing, and posting creates vendor-ledger, general-ledger, and applicable item/tax entries. Payment journals propose or record payments, which are applied and reconciled against open vendor entries. These are cohesive ERP accounting controls rather than simply a supplier-facing profile experience. ([Vendor registration](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/purchasing-how-register-new-vendors); [Posting groups](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/finance-posting-groups); [Purchasing invoices](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/purchasing-how-record-purchases); [Vendor payments](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/payables-make-payments))

Business Central supplies payment blocking states, bank-account change monitoring, a vendor-bank-account approval workflow, selected-field Change Log, and a broad accounts-payable reporting and analysis set. The documentation does **not** establish a universal approval workflow for every vendor-card edit, so qsell should not be judged deficient for lacking that blanket behavior. BC’s standard v2.0 vendor API supports CRUD for a useful but partial field set; bank accounts and other related or unsupported fields may require other endpoints, extensions, or custom APIs. Local tax, e-document, and payment capabilities vary by jurisdiction and installed apps. ([Vendor bank accounts](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/purchasing-how-set-up-vendors-bank-accounts); [Change Log](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/across-log-changes); [AP analytics](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/payables-reports); [Vendor API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/api-reference/v2.0/resources/dynamics_vendor); [API extensibility](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/api-reference/v2.0/))

For version framing, v26 is the 2025 release wave 1 baseline; v29 is the latest published version in the research snapshot dated 2 November 2026. The v29 release notes list Payables Agent purchase-order matching as generally available and vendor-specific self-billing invoice numbering as public preview; neither should be generalized to all tenants or treated as universal vendor-master functionality. The reviewed v26–v29 sources do not show a new supplier self-service portal or a comprehensive vendor-card approval layer arriving in v29. ([BC update 26.0](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/whatsnew/whatsnew-update-26-0); [BC update 29.0](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/whatsnew/whatsnew-update-29-0))

## qsell’s portal and controls already exceed the standard BC onboarding surface

The qsell implementation includes company-scoped vendor creation, activation and lifecycle controls; keyset-paged search; portal ownership and team roles; profile sections, contacts, references, document upload and review; and sealed director-ID and bank-account values. Profile snapshots, event histories, server-side role checks, ownership scoping, and bank-change controls are represented in services and migrations. A dedicated bank-change workflow includes four-eyes control, cool-off, and atomic promotion. The vendor portal also has qualification and document gates, RFQ response/quote flows, purchase-order confirmation, supplier invoice and credit workflows, payment visibility, and team administration. This is meaningful supplier collaboration/self-service functionality that is not documented as part of BC’s standard vendor-card onboarding surface. ([Vendor core audit](../research_notes/Business%20Central%20vendor%20management/qsell-vendor-core-audit.md); [Portal audit](../research_notes/Business%20Central%20vendor%20management/qsell-vendor-portal-audit.md); [Vendor profile service](../src/server/domains/vendors/profile-service.ts); [Invitation service](../src/server/domains/vendors/invitation-service.ts); [Bank-change service](../src/server/domains/vendors/bank-change-service.ts))

qsell further offers substantive controls that should be treated as strengths, not erased by a parity checklist: qualification questionnaires and document validity gates; supplier collaboration on RFQs and quotes; immutable/append-only histories in several domains; sensitive data sealing and masked reads; bank changes segregated from ordinary profile edits; and Kenya-oriented ETIMS purchase/e-invoice alignment. Its portal access derives vendor identity from the authenticated session rather than trusting a client-supplied vendor ID. These protections have meaningful schema, integration-test, and selected browser-test evidence, although test-file existence does not itself establish that all tests currently pass. ([Prequalification service](../src/server/domains/vendors/prequal-service.ts); [Portal access](../src/server/domains/vendors/portal-access.ts); [ETIMS migration](../drizzle/0099_etims_purchase.sql); [Vendor profile integration tests](../src/server/domains/vendors/vendor-profile.integration.test.ts); [Vendor onboarding E2E](../e2e/vendor-onboarding.spec.ts))

The significant qsell gaps are concentrated in ERP breadth and a subset of explicitly deferred procurement controls. BC provides deeper standard purchase-to-pay accounting, posting setup, payment journal and reconciliation, multiple remit-to/order addresses, and mature AP reports; qsell’s audited vendor domain should not be represented as full BC parity in those areas. Within qsell, the supplier invoice model requires a single PO and linked PO lines, excluding multi-PO and free-standing expense invoices described in design. RFQ gaps include attachments, weighted evaluation, foreign-currency comparison, issue/award approvals, award-to-PO conversion, and branch/dimensions. Qualification decisions are not connected to the approvals engine, the file-question type and country-specific tax-ID pattern are not built, and some service-invoice acceptance/matching is simplified. These are real but bounded gaps; other architecture “not built” lists conflict with current service/test surfaces for contracts, landed costs, requisitions, scorecards, and EDI, and require verification before being repeated as current status. ([Core audit](../research_notes/Business%20Central%20vendor%20management/qsell-vendor-core-audit.md); [Purchase invoice process in BC](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/purchasing-how-record-purchases); [AP reports in BC](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/payables-reports))

## Diagnose profile editing and invitation email at the point of failure

The repository contradicts the broad claim that vendor profile updates do not exist: profile forms expose section saves and the service enforces owner/role and lifecycle rules; the onboarding E2E journey saves sections, submits for review, and exercises revision/resubmission. It also intentionally limits editability: review can lock changes, approved vendors can maintain only selected details, and bank/payment data uses a separate governed change process. A read-only team member, wrong vendor association, pending/archived state, review lock, or use of the ordinary profile screen for bank changes can therefore explain a blocked update. The precise user’s failure remains unknown without the page/action result and account/vendor state. Support should check authenticated email, active `vendor_users` association and role, vendor status/block, profile/review state, and relevant event history before concluding there is a product defect. ([Profile actions](../src/server/domains/vendors/profile-actions.ts); [Profile service](../src/server/domains/vendors/profile-service.ts); [Vendor onboarding E2E](../e2e/vendor-onboarding.spec.ts))

Invitation-led onboarding is already implemented for staff-invited owners and owner-invited colleagues, including role checks, one-time hashed auth tokens, a seven-day validity, and tests that follow invitation through password setting and portal access. But `/vendor/register` is also publicly available and creates vendor businesses; this is a documented intentional design choice, not an absent capability. To match the requested policy, disable that public business-registration route/action while retaining staff vendor creation, vendor login, and private invitation acceptance. Do not delete existing registrations as part of the policy change. Existing email addresses are another known edge: the current invitation path silently does not associate or invite an already-registered account, so an explicit authenticated “accept invitation” flow is needed rather than unsafe automatic attachment or account-existence disclosure. ([Invitation service](../src/server/domains/vendors/invitation-service.ts); [Public registration action](../src/server/domains/vendors/portal-actions.ts); [Invitation tests](../src/server/domains/vendors/vendor-controls.integration.test.ts); [Portal audit](../research_notes/Business%20Central%20vendor%20management/qsell-vendor-portal-audit.md))

The source-level email finding is specific: the DI container selects SMTP only when `EMAIL_DRIVER=smtp`; otherwise it selects `ConsoleEmailAdapter`, which logs a recipient, subject, and body preview and does not deliver mail. This makes console mode a plausible explanation for an email that appears test-like or is absent from the inbox. It does **not** prove the deployed environment uses console mode, nor does the reviewed invitation template contain hard-coded demo text. SMTP sender defaults can also differ from the qsell example configuration, and invitation send failures are caught after the invitation state is persisted. Confirm the deployed driver, host/credentials, sender/from domain, public URL, provider delivery/suppression logs, and a received message’s headers/content before diagnosing the reported email. ([Container email selection and adapter behavior](../src/server/di/container.ts); [SMTP adapter](../src/server/adapters/email/nodemailer.adapter.ts); [Vendor notifier](../src/server/domains/vendors/notifier.ts); [.env.example](../.env.example))

## Prioritize policy, reliable delivery, then procurement parity

The backlog below sequences operational verification before changes that alter onboarding, followed by durable invitation controls, profile clarity, and broader procurement coverage. Status terms are evidence-qualified: **built** means source and meaningful tests exist, not that this audit ran them; **partial/deferred** means some material behavior is absent or constrained; **documented but unverified** means architecture claims or file inventory need implementation/runtime confirmation.

| Sequence | Priority and status | Proposed work |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Built; deployment behavior unverified** | Reproduce the reported profile and invitation journey using the actual URL, account, role, vendor state, browser result, and event history. Inspect runtime `EMAIL_DRIVER`, SMTP/provider logs, sender, and public base URL; test a branded message through staging. This distinguishes access/lifecycle blocks from UI defects and mail misconfiguration. |
| 1 | **Built policy mismatch** | Adopt invitation-led entry: remove public vendor self-registration from routes/navigation and stop the public business-creation action, retaining staff-created vendors and invitation/login entry. Define treatment of existing self-registered vendors and pending identities. |
| 2 | **Partial: invitation persistence/delivery** | Model invitations as explicit records with state, expiry, revocation, role, normalized email, inviter, invitee, and hashed token; use invitation-specific acceptance rather than password-reset purpose. Enqueue delivery transactionally through a durable outbox with bounded retries and visible queued/sent/failed/resend status. Keep public responses enumeration-safe. |
| 3 | **Partial: existing accounts and concurrency** | Add authenticated acceptance for an existing account matching the invited email; make acceptance, role/link creation, and invite consumption atomic. Protect active-owner and member-cap rules under concurrent invitations, define revoke/removal/session invalidation behavior, and audit all transitions. |
| 4 | **Built capability; user diagnosis and UX need verification** | Make profile editability understandable at each lifecycle state: show role and review lock, available sections, and the correct bank-change route; return actionable, non-sensitive errors. Verify after approval, during review, under readonly/finance/sales roles, and after revision. Do not make sensitive bank edits ordinary profile saves. |
| 5 | **Partial/deferred procurement and finance breadth** | Decide requirements for multi-PO and non-PO expense invoices; complete RFQ attachments/addenda, weighted scoring, currency conversion, issue/award approvals, award-to-PO conversion, and branch/dimensions where needed. Integrate qualification decisions with approvals and complete service-acceptance/matching policy. Treat BC posting, payment journal/reconciliation, localization, and AP reporting as separate parity workstreams, not implied by portal completion. |
| 6 | **Documented but unverified in parts** | Re-audit payment/provider idempotency and concurrency, batch execution and callbacks, profile submission races, archive-versus-new-payable race, event completeness, and file-size limits. Confirm services for contracts, landed costs, requisitions, scorecards, and EDI against routes, permissions, migrations, and end-to-end behavior before updating feature claims. |
| 7 | **Evidence and documentation gap** | Reconcile the vendor architecture’s stale opening “Nothing here is built yet” and deferred lists against current implementation. Publish a current built/partial/deferred/unverified matrix. Add/locate API-boundary tests and complete the project-mandated unit, database integration, API, and desktop/mobile E2E evidence for changed workflows. Run against fresh dedicated test databases; this audit ran no tests. |

## Conclusion

The best comparison is not “qsell lacks vendor management”: qsell has a substantial supplier-facing lifecycle and governance layer, while BC remains stronger as a configurable accounting and purchasing system. The immediate product issue is to align entry policy with invitation-led onboarding and make role/state restrictions and delivery status observable; the immediate operational question is whether production email configuration matches the repository’s SMTP example. Address those with evidence before broad parity work. The worktree already contains extensive unrelated delivery-module implementation and changes; this report is the only requested deliverable, and no application code or database changes were made.
