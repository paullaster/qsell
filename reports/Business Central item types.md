# Model item types as operational contracts

Business Central (BC) has achieved the item-type distinction by making it an operational contract that gates which inventory, costing, warehouse, planning, tracking, and reservation workflows an item can enter—not a user-defined product label or a synonym for sellability. From application version 26.0 onward, the reviewed release inventories for versions 26, 27, 28, and 29 document adjacent improvements but no change to the three Item Card Type choices: **Inventory, Non-Inventory, and Service**. This is a substantive mismatch with qsell today: qsell's ItemType classifies items and controls sellability, while product rows all carry stock and reservation fields and order flows price and reserve lines without a type-level operational gate. A BC-inspired model should separate operational item kind from commercial sellability, tax-authority classification, catalog lifecycle, and packaging role, then enforce each kind's capabilities at every write boundary. The conclusions below distinguish Microsoft's documented behavior from architectural recommendations and tenant-specific details.

## BC keeps three types because they have different ledger consequences

Microsoft defines Inventory as a physical or identifiable item whose quantity and value are tracked; Non-Inventory as a physical item deliberately not managed in inventory; and Service as a labor/time or other service unit. All can appear in sales and purchasing, but a shared Item Card and document line do not imply shared stock effects. The feature matrix makes Inventory the full operational type; Non-Inventory and Service do not track inventory quantity or value. ([Understand item types](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-about-item-types); [Create item cards for goods or services](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-register-new-items))

| Capability | Inventory | Non-Inventory | Service |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales and purchasing | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Consumption in jobs/service | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Assembly/production consumption | Yes | Yes | No |
| Output, transfers, physical counts, revaluation | Yes | No | No |
| Inventory costing and stock quantity/value | Yes | No | No |
| Item tracking, reservations, warehouse | Yes | No | No |
| Normal planning | Yes | No | No |
| Order planning | Yes | Yes | Yes |

The table summarizes the current Microsoft item-type feature matrix; a “yes” means the matrix lists support, not that every transaction is automatic or enabled without setup. ([Understand item types](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-about-item-types))

Item setup supplies the context around Type rather than replacing it: templates can initialize cards, while units of measure, replenishment method, planning parameters, warehouse settings, item tracking policy, costing method, vendor data, and prices serve distinct purposes. The item card's replenishment and planning fields do not make a Service or Non-Inventory item stock-bearing. Type is therefore a capability boundary; local posting groups, location/setup, and extensions still determine transaction-level details. ([Create item cards for goods or services](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-register-new-items); [Posting group setup](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/finance-posting-groups))

## Stock, value, and posting follow only the inventory lifecycle

For Inventory items, receipts, shipments, transfers, journals, and other inventory events create quantity changes in Item Ledger Entries and value changes in Value Entries. Cost adjustment applies inbound costs to outbound transactions; inventory cost can post automatically or through the Adjust Cost and Post Inventory Cost to G/L processes, with reconciliation between the inventory subledger and general ledger. Inventory posting setup and posting groups map these values to G/L accounts; Type alone does not specify a universal account. ([Managing inventory](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-manage-inventory); [Managing inventory costs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/finance-manage-inventory-costs); [Reconcile inventory costs with the general ledger](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/finance-how-to-post-inventory-costs-to-the-general-ledger); [Posting group setup](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/finance-posting-groups))

Non-Inventory and Service costs are recorded as non-inventory costs and are not reconciled to G/L as inventory costs. They can still have commercial prices, purchase or sales costs, and G/L effects; “no inventory cost” does not mean “no accounting.” Exact revenue, expense, tax, and posting timing depend on company setup and localization. ([Understand item types](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-about-item-types); [Posting group setup](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/finance-posting-groups))

The distinction propagates into fulfillment. Inventory supports the inventory-ledger flows, availability, physical counting, warehouse execution, and the full tracking/reservation set. Serial, lot, and package tracking can be assigned on inbound/outbound documents and carried into item ledger entries. By contrast, Microsoft's warehouse-pick guidance says non-inventory lines such as Service items do not produce inventory-pick entries; service orders have their own limitations in the basic inventory-pick flow. A Service-type item is not thereby a Service Management “service item”: the latter is a customer-owned object being repaired or maintained, and its components can be regular inventory items. ([Track items with serial, lot, and package numbers](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-work-item-tracking); [How to pick items with inventory picks](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/warehouse-how-to-pick-items-with-inventory-picks); [Set up service items and service item components](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/service-how-setup-service-items))

Lifecycle controls preserve historical meaning. A regular item with posted transactions cannot be deleted through the normal workflow because its ledger history is needed for valuation and audit. Catalog items are a separate pre-master workflow: choosing one from a sales quote/order/blanket order converts it into a regular item using catalog data and a template; catalog items are not a fourth Item Card Type and are not selectable directly on a sales invoice. An automatically created catalog item can be removed with its originating line only while it has not been used in protected business references or ledger entries. Microsoft's end-user material does not establish a general safe conversion of an already-used regular item between the three types, so such a migration should not be assumed. ([Create item cards for goods or services](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-register-new-items); [Create and manage catalog items](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-how-work-nonstock-items); [Understand item types](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-about-item-types))

## Releases 26–29 improve adjacent workflows, not the type taxonomy

The official version What's New inventories reviewed for 26.0, 27.0, 28.0, and 29.0 do not announce a new Item Card Type or a revised three-type capability matrix. This is a finding about published release summaries, **not proof that no internal base-application code changed**; Microsoft's summaries are not exhaustive enum or validation diffs. ([26.0 What's New](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/whatsnew/whatsnew-update-26-0); [27.0 What's New](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/whatsnew/whatsnew-update-27-0); [28.0 What's New](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/whatsnew/whatsnew-update-28-0); [29.0 What's New](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/whatsnew/whatsnew-update-29-0); [current item-type matrix](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/inventory-about-item-types))

The relevant adjacent changes are precise and do not imply taxonomy changes. Version 26 lists optional concurrent posting of item ledger and value entries (disabled by default in 26.0), project-item receipts/put-aways, manufacturing workflow changes, and cost-adjustment troubleshooting. Version 27 lists sales/purchase posting line sorting in public preview and makes the feature update for package tracking in physical inventory orders mandatory; that is a rollout change to package-tracking workflow, not creation of package tracking or an item type. Version 28 lists item-variant images/attributes and Shopify product-option/catalog improvements alongside inventory analytics and posting/procurement changes. Version 29 lists subcontracting/warehouse and manufacturing workflow improvements, quality inspection, and sustainability/carbon accounting features around item transactions and tracking. These are changes around posting, catalog presentation, workflow, and analytics, not evidence that Service or Non-Inventory acquired Inventory semantics. ([26.0 What's New](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/whatsnew/whatsnew-update-26-0); [27.0 What's New](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/whatsnew/whatsnew-update-27-0); [28.0 What's New](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/whatsnew/whatsnew-update-28-0); [29.0 What's New](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/whatsnew/whatsnew-update-29-0))

## Qsell currently models classification, not BC operational type

The source observations show `item_types` has a tenant-defined name, `isSellable`, and an integer `code` documented in the schema as a tax-authority code. `products.itemTypeId` references that classification and denormalizes `isSellable`; every product row also has stock/reserved quantities and stock/cost fields. `CatalogService` uses the selected type to require a price for sellable products, but this is a commercial rule. In the order path, `OrdersWriteService` prices lines and calls `InventoryReservationService`; the reservation service reserves submitted product quantities without an Inventory/Non-Inventory/Service operational-kind gate. These observations describe the current code paths, not a claim that every order route always reaches the same branch.

That is a substantive model mismatch, not a naming difference. In BC, choosing Service or Non-Inventory excludes the item from stock quantity/value and inventory reservation, tracking, warehouse, and normal planning capabilities. In qsell, item type presently decides sellability while the product schema and reservation flow assume inventory-bearing products. Consequently a service or non-stock purchasable/sellable good cannot be represented with BC-equivalent operational invariants merely by adding labels to existing user-defined types. Conversely, a raw material that is not customer-sellable can still be an Inventory item; sellability and inventory participation are independent decisions.

Checkout packaging is already explicitly identified in qsell as a checkout-only role on an inventory-bearing product and is filtered from ordinary catalog browsing. That is directionally right: packaging is not a substitute for item kind. Keep packaging role orthogonal to operational kind, and define whether that role is sellable, stock-managed, and reservable through explicit constraints; current behavior requiring sellability does not itself establish the right inventory semantics for every packaging use case.

## A BC-inspired target separates kind, commerce, and lifecycle

**Recommendation (architectural inference):** introduce a constrained operational kind—`inventory`, `non_inventory`, `service`—on the product/item master, independently retain `isSellable` (or an explicit sales policy), and move the tax-authority code into an explicitly named compliance classification. Keep category, user-defined attributes, units, and checkout-packaging role as separate dimensions. Add separate catalog/pre-master state only if qsell needs BC-like catalog conversion; do not encode it as `non_inventory`.

The operational kind should gate capabilities server-side, including API/import writes: only Inventory products can receive stock balances, reservations, inventory movements, item tracking, and warehouse fulfillment; Service lines can be priced and sold without stock deduction; Non-Inventory lines can be traded without stock availability or inventory-cost reconciliation. Inventory balances should be derived from durable movements/reservations, with location and tracking dimensions where those workflows are supported. Commercial document lines can remain shared, but posting should branch explicitly by kind and preserve the item's kind and accounting decision as transaction-time evidence. Prevent destructive deletion or silent reclassification once durable references exist; block/retire or create a governed successor instead. The precise posting-account model is qsell's design decision and should not be inferred from BC's item type alone.

The practical migration is to audit existing types and products, then map operational intent separately from sellability and authority codes. Assign Inventory only where qsell's stock ledger and reservation behavior is intended; classify service offerings and deliberately unstocked goods separately, and preserve existing catalog/tax mappings. Validate every sales, checkout, purchase, import, fulfillment, cancellation, and reporting path against the kind contract before enabling the new distinction. This is a recommended target, not a claim that qsell already enforces it.

## Conclusion

BC's achievement is the end-to-end consistency between one master-data choice and the transaction capabilities, ledgers, costing, warehouse, planning, and lifecycle rules that follow. The key design implication for qsell is to make operational kind constrain those paths, while keeping sellability, tax classification, catalog state, and packaging independently expressible. Version 29 remains consistent with the three-type model in Microsoft's published material as of 2026-11-01; tenant localization, feature configuration, extensions, and exact build can affect edge behavior, so accounting-critical parity still requires validation in the target BC tenant and build.
